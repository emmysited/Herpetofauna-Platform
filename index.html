Why herpetofauna?

Reptiles and amphibians face habitat loss, climate change, road mortality, illegal trade and persecution, and fear and misinformation make it worse.

Frequently asked questions

How do I join a class?

Open the Classes page, create a free account, then choose your class and follow the curriculum.

How do I report a sighting?

Use the Report a sighting page. Tell us the animal, place and date, and add a photo if you can. Never touch or chase a wild animal.

What should I do if someone is bitten by a snake?

Keep the person calm and still, and take them to the nearest health facility immediately. Do not cut the wound, suck the venom or tie anything tightly.

How do I book a tour?

Send us a message on the Contact page and we will confirm prices and dates.

Can my school, company or organisation partner with us?

Yes. Contact us and tell us about your organisation.

Privacy policy

Draft, to be reviewed before launch.

We collect only what you give us: your name and email when you create a class account, the details you send in a sighting report, and the messages you send us by WhatsApp or email.

Citizen science: report a sighting

Saw a snake, lizard, frog or crocodile? Tell us where. Your records help document biodiversity. Roadkill reports are welcome too.

Safety first: never touch or chase a wild animal. Take a photo from a safe distance.

Snake safety and learning

Education that reduces fear and saves lives, human and animal.

  1. Stay calm and give space. Most snakes want to escape, not attack.
  2. Never try to kill or handle it. Most bites happen when people do.
  3. Keep yards tidy. Clear tall grass, rubbish and wood piles that hide rodents and snakes.
  4. If bitten, go to a health facility immediately. Keep the person still and calm, and do not cut, suck or tie the wound.
  5. Call a trained snake handler or wildlife authority to move the animal safely.
